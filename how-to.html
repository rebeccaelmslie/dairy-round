How to use it

Dairy Round — a walk through your first visit

The idea

Everything you record at one farm on one day belongs to a single visit. The farm walk, the condition scoring, the herd figures, the supplements and the feed on hand all attach to it. That is what lets the app put this month beside last month, or beside the same week last year, without you filing anything.

You do not create a visit. Start recording and one is opened for today; carry on tomorrow and a new one begins.

Setting up a farm

You do this once per client. After that, a visit is just the recording.

1

Add the client

Tap + on the Clients screen. A last name is all that is required — phone, email and business name can wait.

2

Add the farm and its paddocks

Open the client and tap Farm Details. Add a block — most farms need only one, but a runoff or a lease block can be added separately. Then bulk-add the paddocks.

Paddocks keep their identity, so a paddock scored low this month is the same paddock next month.

3

Add the herds

Stock Management holds the mobs — milkers, springers, young stock. Give each a name and a number; both can be edited later.

Recording a visit

4

Walk the farm

Pasture Cover takes a cover per paddock as you drive round. You can stop part way and pick it up later in the day — it is the same walk until you leave it.

Covers are typed in hundreds: type 25 for 2500 KgDM/ha. Anything that looks like a slip is queried before it reaches your average.

5

Score the cows

Open a herd and tap BCS. One tap is one cow. Keep tapping as they come past; the distribution builds as you go and Undo last fixes a mis-tap.

6

Enter the herd's figures

Feeding takes the stock number, the area allocated, the pre- and post-grazing covers and the round length, plus supplements in KgDM per cow per day.

Supplements carry forward from the previous visit until you change them, so a herd on the same ration needs no re-entry.

7

Measure what is in the stack

Feed On Hand holds silage, balage and concentrate. Re-measure a stack and the app reports what has been eaten since the last reading.

Bale weights are entered as KgDM per bale, not fresh weight — a "180 kg bale" in conversation usually means DM. There is a DM% field if you are working from a contractor's docket instead.

8

Read it back

A herd's summary shows this visit beside the previous one — covers, intake, condition and daily surplus. Previous, Last year and Pick… choose what it compares against.

Visit Summary lists the visits for a client, and the share button turns one into a single-page PDF for your report or for the farmer.

Reading the screens

Fields tell you what they are by how they look:

Looks likeMeans
White box, navy border You can type in it. A dash means it is empty, not locked.
Grey box, dashed border The app works this one out from the others.
No box at all A figure carried in from elsewhere — change it where it was entered.

Where a figure cannot be worked out yet, the app names what is missing rather than showing a dash — for example Needs Allocated area, or Needs 3 figures when several are absent. Enter them on the Feeding screen for that herd and the figure appears.

Backing up

Your records live on the phone and nowhere else. No server holds a copy, which is why the app keeps working in a valley — and why a backup is the only way to survive a lost phone.

Once a round, at the kitchen table:

  1. Tap the backup button at the bottom of the Clients screen.
  2. Back up now writes a copy onto the phone.
  3. Send backup puts a copy somewhere else — iCloud Drive, or emailed to yourself. This is the step that actually protects you.

Each backup writes two files: a .json that restores everything, and a .csv you can open in a spreadsheet.

On a new phone, install the app, tap the backup button and choose Restore from a file. You are shown what the file holds before anything is changed.

A backup kept only on the phone will not survive losing the phone. Sending a copy off the device is the part that matters, and it is the step people skip.
